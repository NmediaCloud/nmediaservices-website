The tedium of selling something used is not the listing. It is the repetition: identify what it actually is, find out what it goes for, write a description that does not read like a template, then type all of it into three forms with three different layouts. The repetition is what stops things getting listed at all.

01What it does

A product starts as a few photographs. From there the pipeline identifies it, researches it, recommends a price and writes the listing. A local agent then opens each marketplace in your own browser and fills in the create-listing form. What it does not do is press Publish.

02It tells you what it does not know

The first step is identification. The model reads the photos and returns what it thinks the item is, with a confidence score and the details it could see: type, brand, model, condition, colour, visible damage.

The part that matters is the yellow box. Battery life, included accessories and remaining warranty cannot be read off a photograph, so instead of guessing it asks. Every AI field is marked as a suggestion rather than a confirmed fact, and every one of them is editable.

04The listing, and the line it will not cross

Everything up to Publish is reversible. Publish is not: it is public, it is attached to your account, and a wrong price or a misidentified product becomes a real-world consequence. So the automation ends exactly where the irreversible part begins. The agent fills the form and stops on the review screen.

"Automate the repetition. Hand back the decision."

05Why the agent runs on your machine

The thinking happens in the web application: identification, research, pricing and listing text. The browser work happens in a small Playwright agent on your own computer, using your own logged-in session. Facebook, Kijiji and Karrot logins stay in a browser profile on your PC, never on a server.

06What it will never do

It will not solve a CAPTCHA, complete two-factor authentication or get around any other platform security. It will not press the final button, store a marketplace password, invent specifications or accessories, hide damage, or mass-message buyers. Those are not missing features. They are the boundary that makes the rest of it safe to use.

Have a repetitive workflow that ends in a form?

The pattern here carries: automate everything reversible, and hand the irreversible step back to a person.
